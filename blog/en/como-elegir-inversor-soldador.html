How to choose a welding inverter

Buying guides · Welding inverters · 4 min read

Amperage, duty cycle, voltage and accessories: what to check before buying.

What it is and why it's used

A welding inverter uses electronics to convert the current and deliver a stable arc. It's usually lighter and more efficient than traditional transformer welders, which makes it practical in the shop and on site.

What to check when buying

  • Maximum amperage: sets the electrode you can use (see the catalog calculator)
  • Duty cycle: the percentage of each 10 minutes the machine can weld at a given current without overheating; more duty cycle, more continuity
  • Supply voltage: 110 V, 220 V or dual; at 110 V the available current is usually lower
  • Processes: stick on all of them; TIG or MIG only on models that state it
  • Overheat and overcurrent protection
  • Accessories: electrode holder, ground clamp, cables, helmet and electrodes

Don't forget safety

Weld with a helmet with a suitable filter, gloves and clothing that covers your skin, closed footwear and good ventilation. See also our safety equipment guide.

Recommended kit for this job

  • Welding inverters
  • 4-1/2"–5" angle grinder 1500W (DWE4314-B3)
  • Safety equipment
